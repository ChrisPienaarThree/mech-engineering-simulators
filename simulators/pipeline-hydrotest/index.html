    Strength test after construction (841.32)

    at least 2 h, for lines to operate at 30 % SMYS or more (841.321)

    Test pressure by location class (841.322, Table 841.322(f)): Class 1 Division 1 water 1.25 × design pressure; Division 2 air, gas or water 1.1 × MAOP; Class 2 air or water 1.25 × MAOP; Classes 3 and 4 water 1.40 × MAOP. The MAOP is the lesser of the design pressure and the test pressure divided by that factor. Choose the test level knowing that it caps the MAOP if the location class later changes (841.322(f), 854).

    Test sections and elevation (Appendix N2)

    p(x) = phead + ρ g (zhead − z(x))  ·  0.0981 bar per metre of water

    The minimum test pressure must be reached at the high point; the low point sees the most pressure. Split the line where the elevation range would push the low point past the hoop limit. Each section is filled, pressurised and held separately.

    Filling (N3)

    Fill continuously behind one or more pigs or spheres to keep air out, meter the water, and let the water temperature settle to the ground before the hold. Trapped air is compressible: the first bars take many strokes and a small leak is harder to see.

    Pressurising (N4)

    to ≤ 80 % of test pressure → hold, look for major leaks → uniform rate to test pressure with a P–V plot → hold, adding water as needed to keep the minimum

    Above 400 psig (27.6 bar) keep people not involved out of the test area (N2(a)); from 50 % SMYS up to the test stress and back to MAOP, keep persons not working on the test out (841.36).

    Finding yield (N5)

    yield: strokes per bar = 2 × the straight-line value  ·  or deviation volume = 0.002 × fill volume

    Below the elastic limit, volume and pressure follow a straight line (pipe expansion plus water compressibility). When pipe somewhere in the section starts to yield, each bar takes more water. Above 100 % SMYS in a section, one of these two rules limits the test pressure (N5(c)).

    Temperature or leak? (N6)

    ΔP ≈ (βwater − 2αsteel) / (D(1−ν²)/(E t) + κwater) · ΔT  ≈ 2 to 3 bar per °C at 15–25 °C

    Water expands far more than steel, so a degree of warming raises the pressure by bars. Correct the hold pressure for the temperature change before deciding: a leak is the change you cannot explain. If a leak is shown, reduce pressure, find and repair it, then start a new hold at full test pressure.

    Records (N7)

    Test medium, pressure, duration, date, pressure chart and log, P–V plot, pressures at the high and low points, elevation of the gauge, people, weather, pipe data, what each section contains, leaks and failures. The data log below holds most of these.

    About the numbers

    [Illustrative data, for training only.] Clause numbers follow ASME B31.8-2003 (the edition in the course folder). The route, the actual yield strengths, the leaks, the damage and the material model (bilinear with 4 GPa hardening, burst at 1.25 × actual yield) are invented. Water properties are fitted curves good to a few per cent between 4 and 35 °C.

    How to use the test bench

    • Plan the sections: the profile shows 24 km of line with 380 m of relief. Click the profile to add or remove section breaks. The lower chart shows whether the high point can reach the minimum test pressure without the low point passing the hoop limit.
    • Fill the selected section, then Pressurise to 80 % and hold to look for big leaks, then Pressurise to test. Watch the P–V plot: a straight line means elastic pipe.
    • Hold for at least 2 hours with the high point above the minimum. Use the clock to speed time up. Top up with Pump to target if the pressure falls.
    • Judge the hold: correct the pressure change for the temperature change, then Accept the test or Reject it for a leak.
    • Record each section. The scenarios load faults to find: air, cold ground, sun, leaks, yielding pipe and construction damage.
