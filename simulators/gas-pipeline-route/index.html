    Steel pipe design formula (841.11)

    P = 2 S t F E T / D  ·  t = P D / (2 S F E T)

    S is the specified minimum yield strength, never the higher actual value (841.112(c)). The design factor F already allows for the mill under-thickness tolerance, so t is the nominal wall. The formula wall is for pressure only: handling, transport, test water and soil loads may need more (841.113(a)), which is why companies set a minimum wall or a maximum D/t. The model then rounds up to the next standard wall.

    Location class (840.21, 840.22)

    zone ¼ mile wide × 1 mile long, slid along the route to catch the most buildings

    Count every building intended for human occupancy in the zone; each dwelling unit in a block of flats counts separately. 10 or fewer: Class 1. 11 to 45: Class 2. 46 or more: Class 3. Multistorey buildings (four or more floors) prevalent: Class 4. A Class 2 or 3 cluster may end 660 ft from the nearest building (840.21(b)). Plan for likely future development (840.42).

    Design factors (Tables 841.114A and B)

    Class 1 Div 1 0.80 · Div 2 0.72 · Class 2 0.60 · Class 3 0.50 · Class 4 0.40

    Exceptions lower F locally: uncased crossings of hard-surface roads and railways 0.60 in Class 1 and 0.50 in Class 2; metering and pressure-control facilities and fabricated assemblies 0.60; compressor station piping 0.50; pipelines near concentrations of 20 or more people (schools, churches, hospitals, organised recreation) in Class 1 or 2 must meet Class 3, F 0.50 (840.3, Table 841.114B).

    Joint and temperature factors (Tables 841.115A, 841.116A)

    E = 1.00 seamless, ERW, EFW, SAW · 0.60 furnace butt weld  ·  T = 1.000 to 121 °C, 0.967 at 149, 0.933 at 177, 0.900 at 204, 0.867 at 232 °C

    Interpolate between temperatures. Above 232 °C the table stops, and the model flags the design as outside it.

    Test pressure (Table 841.322(f))

    water: Class 1 Div 1 1.25 · Div 2 1.1 · Class 2 1.25 · Class 3 and 4 1.40 × MAOP

    The MAOP is the lesser of the design pressure and the test pressure divided by the factor. The test pressure is recorded when you press Build: it matters again when buildings appear later.

    When the location class changes (854, Table 854.1(c))

    existing Class 1 line: 11–25 buildings keep MAOP ≤ 72 % SMYS (Div 2) · 26–45 → 0.800 × test pressure ≤ 72 % · 46–65 → 0.667 × TP ≤ 60 % · 66+ → 0.667 × TP ≤ 60 % · multistorey → 0.555 × TP ≤ 50 %

    An existing line does not have to be redesigned for every new house. Table 854.1(c) gives a tolerance band, then caps the MAOP by the original test pressure and a hoop-stress limit. If that cap is below the operating MAOP, the operator lowers the MAOP (with relief or limiting devices, 854.3) or replaces the pipe with pipe designed to Table 841.114A for the new class (854.2(d)). A new school or similar facility near a Class 1 or 2 line limits the hoop stress to 50 % SMYS unless the line is retested and managed as in 855.1(b).

    Fracture control (841.11(c))

    A fracture toughness criterion or crack arrestors are required above 40 % SMYS for NPS 16 (DN 400) and larger, and above 72 % SMYS for smaller pipe.

    Potential impact radius (ASME B31.8S)

    r = 0.69 · d · √p  (r ft, d in, p psig)  ·  in SI: r [m] ≈ 0.0315 · D [mm] · √P [bar]

    The radius within which a rupture fire could cause death or injury. B31.8S uses it to find high consequence areas: Class 3 and 4 locations, or circles that hold an identified site or many buildings. [Not in B31.8 itself: check the PIR and HCA criteria against your copy of B31.8S.]

    Which rules apply here?

    South Africa: a NERSA licence under the Gas Act 48 of 2001 and the Piped-Gas Regulations 2007; the licence lists the codes, normally ASME B31.8 or ISO 13623, with the OHS Act regulations. Mozambique: licensing under the Petroleum Law 21/2014 and the Petroleum Operations Regulations (Decree 34/2015), regulated by INP; the law requires internationally accepted standards and good oilfield practice, so the design basis names B31.8 or ISO 13623.

    About the numbers

    [Illustrative data, for training only.] Factors, tables and clause numbers follow ASME B31.8-2003, the edition in the course folder. Later editions renumber the clauses (for example 841.1.1 and Table 841.1.6-1) and may change values: check against the edition in force for the project. The route, buildings, D/t handling limit and steel prices are invented.

    How to use the designer

    • Read the route: houses, a village and a town lie along a 12 km line. Move over the map: the dashed box is the 1-mile, ¼-mile-wide class-location unit, and the readout counts the buildings in it.
    • Design: choose pipe size, grade, MAOP, temperature and seam. Every 10 m of route gets a location class, a design factor and a wall from the standard list. The strips under the map show class, F, wall and high consequence areas.
    • Change the route: pick a tool and click the map to add houses, an estate, flats, a school, a road crossing or a station. Erase removes the nearest item.
    • Build and operate: Build freezes the walls and records the test pressure. Then add buildings, or press +5 years, and see whether the MAOP is still allowed by Table 854.1(c). Reduce the MAOP or replace the pipe to comply.
    • Record readings to compare designs and to tick off the learning tasks.
